How this works (read first)

  • Grok Imagine makes short clips (roughly 6–15 seconds each), so a 2-minute film is 14 separate generations edited together, which is also exactly how real launch films are cut.
  • Generate every clip without voice or music (the prompts already say this). You’ll add one consistent narrator voice and one music track in the editor. That’s what makes it sound professional instead of AI-made.
  • Add the words on screen in the editor too, not in Grok. AI video can warp text; your editor won’t.

Alternative frames

🎙 Voiceover

  • Voice: calm, warm, confident, premium, like an Apple keynote narrator. Unhurried, with short pauses between sentences.
  • Easiest: ElevenLabs (elevenlabs.io → Text to Speech). Pick a deep, warm narration voice (search the Voice Library for “narration” or “documentary”). Settings: Stability ~45–55%, Similarity ~75%, Style ~15–25%, Speed 0.9–0.95.
  • Free alternative: CapCut → Text → Text to speech → pick a narrator voice (e.g. “Narrator” / “Calm male”). Or record it yourself on your phone in a quiet room, closet with clothes works great.
  • Generate the voiceover scene by scene (one file per scene) so you can line each line up with its clip.

The full script is in VOICEOVER_SCRIPT.txt.

🎵 Music

  • Minimal, modern cinematic electronic with a soft piano/synth pulse, ~95–105 BPM, Apple-keynote feel. Quiet and moody in S01, lift at “Introducing… Zyro Chat”, steady build through the product scenes, biggest moment at S12–S13, then drop to a single soft note on the end card.
  • Where to get it: CapCut’s built-in music library (search “inspiring technology”, “minimal corporate”, “keynote”), or Artlist / Epidemic Sound / YouTube Audio Library (royalty-free).
  • Mix: music around −18 to −20 dB under the voice (use CapCut’s “Auto ducking”), voice around −6 dB.

✂️ Putting it together

  • 1. In Grok Imagine, pick Video, aspect ratio 16:9, the longest duration it offers (10s or more). For image scenes, upload the image from the frames folder and paste the prompt. Generate 2–3 variations of each scene and keep the cleanest one.
  • 2. Check every clip for AI glitches, especially warped text on screens. If a screen gets distorted, don’t use that clip. Instead, drop the still image straight into your editor and apply a slow zoom (CapCut: select clip → Animation → “Zoom in” or keyframe scale 100% → 108%). It looks just as premium and is 100% sharp.
  • 3. Open CapCut Desktop (free) → New project → set canvas to 16:9, 1080p, 30 fps. Drag the clips in scene order and trim each to the duration in this storyboard (total ≈ 2:00).
  • 4. Add the voiceover files on the audio track under each scene. Start each line ~0.3s after the cut. Add the music underneath with Auto ducking on.
  • 5. Add the on-screen text (column “Text overlay”): white, clean sans-serif (e.g. “SF Pro Display”, “Inter” or CapCut’s “Montserrat”), 60–80 px, centered low third or top-center, Fade in 0.3s / Fade out 0.3s. Keep it to max one line per scene: less is more (that’s the Apple look).
  • 6. Transitions: mostly hard cuts on the music beat; use a short Cross dissolve (0.3–0.4s) only into S02 and into S14. Optional: “Blur” transition into S03.
  • 7. Color: add a very light vignette and keep blacks deep. Don’t over-filter.
  • 8. Export: 1080p (or 4K if you upscale), 30 fps, H.264, high bitrate. File name: Zyro-Chat-Pitch.mp4.
  • 9. In your slides: insert the MP4 on its own slide, set it to play automatically and full-screen. Test it on the exact computer and projector you’ll present with, and keep a copy on a USB stick.
  • 10. Timing for your 5-minute pitch: ~1:00 intro/problem (you talking) → 2:00 video → ~2:00 business model, traction, the ask.
