# Anchor every marketing number to profit

Leading consumer brands don't run marketing from a wall of channel metrics. They run it from **one profit outcome at the top, a handful of business drivers under it, and channel and creative diagnostics at the bottom**, reviewed every week in the same fixed order, with discussion spent on the inputs the team can actually move. For emet, that means the dashboard's headline should be **contribution profit to date against a €15K pacing line**. Under it sit revenue and orders by channel, AOV, margin, repeat rate, and four controllable inputs: posts shipped, content hit rate, share-plus-save rate per reach, and inquiry-to-order conversion. With no ad spend, ROAS, multi-touch attribution and LTV projections are noise. Attribution at €0 comes from three cheap signals checked against each other: UTM links, one discount code per channel, and a mandatory "how did you hear about us" field on every order. On the operations side, the transferable practices are a one-page brief with a single approver, one combined content-and-experiment log that doubles as the "what works" library, a shared tag glossary, and a 30-minute Monday review. The only data worth automating now is the team's own Instagram account (free API, no App Review), Stripe payment webhooks and a self-built link and QR redirect. TikTok's deeper analytics and stall sales stay manual. In the visual layer, every number someone has to act on stays in 2D: bullet graphs, sorted bars, stepped funnels and thumbnail leaderboards. The existing 3D revenue mountain, and possibly a "content landscape", earn their place only as memorable overviews that open into 2D readouts.

## Profit-first hierarchies beat channel dashboards

The clearest operator model comes from the DTC agency Common Thread Collective, which puts **contribution margin at the top of the metric hierarchy**. Below it come business-level metrics (revenue, total spend, MER, AOV, orders, new-customer CAC), and below those channel metrics such as Meta ROAS, email revenue and creative performance. Diagnosis runs top-down: check margin first, then the business metrics, then drill into the channel. As CTC puts it, "if revenue is on track but CM is short, the problem is usually on the cost side." CTC also breaks the annual goal into period targets and tracks pacing against them ([CTC](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/the-complete-guide-to-ecommerce-contribution-margin)). Amazon's Weekly Business Review adds the cadence. It separates **controllable input metrics** from output metrics that leaders "have little direct control over", keeps the same metrics in the same order every week so changes jump out, spends discussion on exceptions in the inputs, and expects the first set of inputs to be wrong and revised over time ([Holistics](https://www.holistics.io/blog/how-amazon-uses-input-metrics/)). AARRR and North Star frameworks (Airbnb's "nights booked" broken into breadth, depth and frequency) are useful vocabulary for funnel stages ([Amplitude](https://www.amplitude.com/blog/good-bad-north-star-metric.md)). For a physical-product seller, though, the profit-first, input/output structure is the one that leads to decisions.

The formulas matter because sources define them differently, and a team that doesn't fix its definitions ends up with numbers it can't compare. CTC defines **CM = (net sales + shipping revenue) − COGS − ad spend − shipping and fulfilment − merchant fees**, excluding fixed costs ([CTC](https://commonthreadco.com/blogs/coachs-corner/the-complete-guide-to-ecommerce-contribution-margin)). **MER is total revenue ÷ total marketing spend**: "ROAS grades a campaign, MER grades a business". **Break-even MER is 1 ÷ CM%** ([Polar Analytics](https://www.polaranalytics.com/post/mer-marketing-efficiency-ratio)). **CAC divides all acquisition costs, including promotional discounts and content production, by new customers only**. Counting repeat orders in the denominator understates it ([Polar Analytics](https://www.polaranalytics.com/post/customer-acquisition-cost-cac)). Shopify defines **CLV as AOV × purchase frequency × lifespan** ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/enterprise/blog/44310083-warning-most-conversion-optimization-tips-are-bs-heres-why)). **Repeat purchase rate** averages 28.2% across ecommerce according to one vendor, ranging from 9.9% in luxury to 65.2% in grocery ([Rivo](https://www.rivo.io/blog/repeat-purchase-rate-complete-guide)). A 60-day repeat rate paired with first-order margin is a practical early proxy for LTV ([Hologrow](https://hologrow.ai/blog/customer-acquisition-quality)).

emet's own targets imply a useful fact: **€15K profit on €30K revenue is a 50% margin, so the break-even MER, once any paid promotion starts, is 2.0**. Every euro of boosted posts must return at least two euros of revenue. Starting from zero in mid-October 2026, the goal works out to roughly **€810 revenue and €405 profit per week** over the ~37 weeks to the end of June 2027. A seasonal curve (gift periods, exam weeks, campus events) is more honest than a straight line. At €0 budget, "marketing spend" is not zero. It is product used for samples and giveaways, discounts given, printing and stall costs. Defining it that way now keeps MER and blended CAC computable from day one and ready when real spend arrives.

| Rank | Metric | Formula / source | Owner | Why it earns a place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Output | Contribution profit vs pace | Σ(gross − discounts − COGS − payment fees − packaging/shipping) − variable marketing €; pace line to €15K | Leader | The only number that defines success |
| Business | Revenue and orders by channel | Orders log; channel = HDYHAU answer, code/UTM as cross-check | Leader | Shows where the money comes from |
| Business | AOV and CM% | Revenue ÷ orders; CM ÷ net revenue | Leader | Stall-vs-online gap shows where bundles help |
| Business | Repeat rate (60-day and all-time) | Customers with ≥2 orders ÷ customers; needs a customer ID on every order | Researcher | Early stand-in for LTV |
| Input | Posts shipped vs planned | Content log | Social media manager | Main controllable lever |
| Input | Hit rate | Posts with views ≥2× trailing-10 median ÷ posts | Creator | Measures creative quality without benchmark noise |
| Input | Share + save rate per reach | (shares + saves) ÷ reach at day 7 | Social media manager | Closest organic signal to the algorithm and to intent |
| Input | Inquiry-to-order rate | Orders from IG/TT ÷ DMs and inquiries | Social media manager | Connects social activity to sales |
| Input | Revenue per stall-hour | Stall log | Leader | Shows whether stalls are worth the people-hours |

The organic metrics in that table follow Instagram's own signals. Adam Mosseri named **watch time, likes and sends as the top three ranking signals**, with sends weighing more for reaching non-followers ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). He also stressed that rates per reach matter more than raw counts. Industry engagement benchmarks are close to useless for a small account. Rival IQ measured year-on-year engagement drops of **16% on Instagram and 34% on TikTok** in its 2025 report ([Rival IQ](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/)). Published medians range from **0.36% to roughly 5%** depending on denominator and sample ([Inrō](https://www.inro.social/tools/instagram-engagement-rate-calculator)). Socialinsider's median for accounts with 1–5K followers is **580 views per Reel and about one save per post** ([Socialinsider](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-hashtag-study/)). emet should therefore compare each post against its own trailing median, expect hundreds of views rather than thousands, and treat follower count, likes, hashtag performance and posting-time tweaks as context rather than goals. No public study links saves or shares directly to sales for small product brands, so ranking DMs above link taps, profile visits, shares and saves as purchase predictors is a reasoned inference, not a measured result.

## Three imperfect attribution signals, checked against each other, replace paid tools

Small brands without pixels or ad-attribution software cross-check three free methods, each covering the others' blind spots. **UTM links** are precise for web traffic but "die when someone closes their browser, clears cookies, or switches devices". **Unique discount codes per channel or creator** catch sales that never touched a link, at the cost of margin and some leakage ([ReferralCandy](https://www.referralcandy.com/blog/how-to-set-up-influencer-affiliate-tracking-on-shopify)). Pairing each code with a UTM campaign name gives a sale two independent confirmation paths, and a fixed `utm_source` vocabulary prevents conversions from falling into "(not set)" ([InfluencerMarketingHub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/utms-promo-codes-holidays-bf-cm)). **Post-purchase "how did you hear about us" surveys** capture word of mouth and dark social (private shares in DMs and group chats) that tracking misses ([Fairing](https://fairing.co/resources/measurement-faqs/what-is-a-post-purchase-survey)). Tracked attribution over-credits the last digital touch, while self-reports under-count brand recall, so each corrects the other ([Ecommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/how-zero-party-attribution-data-fuels-a-competitive-advantage-for-dtc-brands/)). Word of mouth can dominate for this kind of brand: Glossier attributed roughly 70–80% of growth to peer recommendations and owned channels, though those figures are dated and inconsistent ([The Hustle](https://thehustle.co/glossier-raises-52-million)).

For emet the paid survey tools are unnecessary. Fairing starts at $49/month ([Fairing](https://app.fairing.co/powered-by)), but a required dropdown on every order (web checkout, DM order template, stall phone form) does the same job, with fixed options: TikTok, Instagram, friend, campus stall, ambassador, other. Codes follow one pattern (`IG10`, `TT10`, `STALL-<place>`, `AMB-<name>`), and every bio, story and QR link is UTM-tagged with `utm_source` restricted to `ig`, `tt`, `stall` or `ambassador`. When the signals disagree, HDYHAU should decide the channel split for stall and word-of-mouth sales, and codes and UTMs should decide it for online sales. The dashboard should also show the disagreement rate itself, because a rising rate means the tagging discipline is slipping. No independent study measures HDYHAU accuracy, and none gives QR scan-to-purchase rates at pop-up stalls, so emet will have to set its own baselines after 4–6 weeks of logging.

## One brief, one log, one approver: how work reaches each marketer

The best-documented handoff pattern is consistent across sources, even though **no primary 2022–2026 documentation of internal briefs or rituals at Nike, P&G, Red Bull, Airbnb, Glossier, Notion or Linear could be found**. Anything circulating as "Nike's brief" should be treated as unverified. HubSpot's creative brief, mirrored by Atlassian, puts DACI-style roles in the header (driver, approver, contributors, informed). It then covers goal, audience, key message with a reason to believe, tone, pre- and post-exposure behaviour, and a deliverables table of asset, channel, specs and owner ([Atlassian](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/creative-brief)). Asana's own brand team routes every request through **one intake form into one queue triaged by a single head of creative operations**, recording stakeholder roles as a RACI on each request ([Asana](https://asana.com/resources/asana-on-asana-creative-production.md)). The reported gains are vendor case studies and can't be isolated from other changes: Stance says it cut production time by 50% ([Asana: Stance](https://asana.com/case-study/stance-creative-production)). They point the same way, though: speed comes from requests arriving complete. Gymshark grew its in-house studio from 8 to 70+ people and lists "writing briefs for content creators" as a named job skill ([Gymshark](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/gymshark/jobs/4644967101)).

For approvals, Atlassian's DACI rule that **"there must be only one" Approver**, and that contributors have "a voice but no vote", is the single most useful idea for a 15-person startup where every post could otherwise become a group vote ([Atlassian DACI](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci)). Creative testing practice adds two fields that generic briefs lack: a hypothesis and the hook variants to test. Motion's naming convention tags assets with prefixed fields (format, messaging, hook, product, talent, audio, offer). It splits the hook into **hook visual and hook message** and insists on dropdown values from a shared glossary, tracking only "actionable" fields ([Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/build-a-naming-convention-for-success/)). Practitioners recommend **5–6 required tags**, with performance rolled up to the concept rather than to each variant ([Linkrunner](https://deeplinks.linkrunner.io/blog/creative-tagging-taxonomy-that-scales)). True A/B significance is out of reach in organic social because there is no controlled traffic split. The workable approach is to vary only the opening and hold everything else constant ([blckalpaca](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-content-creation-formats/hook-rate-retention-watch-time)). Results are directional, and the dashboard should label them that way. Hook-rate benchmarks of 20–30% are vendor figures using different second thresholds (2 s on TikTok, 3 s on Meta) ([Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate)), so they shouldn't be used as targets.

Adapted to emet, the marketing leader is the sole approver. The brief is a one-page form (in the dashboard or in Notion) filled in by the requester at least three days before posting. Its fields are: ID, DACI line, one measurable goal, audience, a customer quote supplied by the researcher, key message, reason to believe, an if-then hypothesis, 2–3 hook variants of the same body, a deliverables table, reference links from the swipe file, pre-filled tags and mandatories (price, handle, stall time and place). Each role gets a short scorecard of numbers it can move, which is the practical test of whether a metric is vanity:

| Role | Owns | Weekly numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing leader | Goals, approvals, rituals, post-mortems | Contribution profit vs pace, revenue per stall-hour, experiments closed with a decision |
| Social media manager | Calendar, posting, replies, log entries | Posts shipped vs planned, share + save rate per reach, inquiries and inquiry-to-order rate |
| Content creator | Concepts, hooks, scripts, on-camera | Hook rate per variant, hook variants tested, hit rate |
| Editor / designer | Edits, covers, stall signage, tag glossary | Turnaround vs due date, hold rate by edit style |
| Market researcher | Customer-language bank, competitor swipe file, results read-outs | Insights logged, share of briefs citing an insight, repeat rate |

The rituals stay light. A **30-minute Monday review** walks the fixed scorecard (10 minutes deciding scale, iterate or kill on last week's posts, 10 minutes approving this week's briefs, 5 minutes on research insights, 5 minutes on blockers). A **60-minute monthly retro** reviews post-mortems, prunes the library and sets one or two plain monthly targets. Full OKRs are optional, since no source compares OKRs with simpler goal-setting for teams this small. The researcher's most valuable output, judging by Glossier, is a continuous stream of customer language: Glossier shaped a cleanser from 400+ community comments, tracking recurring words like "glowy" and "mild" ([MBS Group](https://www.thembsgroup.co.uk/internal/glossier-beautiful-on-the-inside-too/)). That stream should feed every brief, rather than appearing in one survey.

## The experiment log becomes the playbook once every row carries a decision

The "what works" knowledge base that mature teams build has three parts that share one tag vocabulary. The first is a **post-mortem stored with the campaign record**, summary on top, which Dropbox says should "live with the campaign record, not in a one-off deck that disappears after the meeting" ([Dropbox](https://www.dropbox.com/resources/campaign-post-mortem)). It should compare results against targets set before launch ([Product Marketing Alliance](https://productmarketingalliance.com/content/files/2025/01/Product-launch-post-mortem-template.pdf)), and it should be shared even when the campaign flopped ([Zappi](https://www.zappi.io/web/blog/advertising-postmortems-make-next-campaign-better)). The second is a **tagged swipe file** of reference creatives. Tools like Foreplay (vendor-described, roughly $59+/month and unconfirmed) save ads from the Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center and turn patterns into briefs ([Foreplay](https://www.foreplay.co)). The third is a **performance log of the team's own assets**, grouped by tag. Generic experiment-log templates share the fields ID, if-then hypothesis, success metric, duration, result and learnings ([Appcues](https://appcues.com/templates/growth-experiment-template)). They usually miss the field that turns a log into a playbook: an explicit **decision of scale, iterate or kill**.

emet should therefore not build a separate "library". One table, a row per published asset, carries the concept ID, brief ID, tags, the single variable changed, metrics at 48 hours and 7 days, a baseline (trailing 10-post median), result, a one-sentence learning and the decision. A "Library" view filtered to *decision = scale* and grouped by hook, format or product is the playbook, and it updates itself every Monday. The glossary, kept by the editor, fixes six required dropdown tags: `p:` product, `f:` format, `hv:` hook visual, `hm:` hook message, `aud:` audience, `ch:` channel. Optional tags are `o:` offer, `a:` audio and `t:` talent. The swipe file is the same table with a "competitor/reference" flag, stocked in a weekly manual session. The known weakness, which Segwise flags, is that tags only capture what someone typed at launch and people fill in the easy fields ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/how-to-tag-ad-creatives-automatically)). Making the tags required dropdowns on the brief, so they carry over into the log, is the fix. Each stall day or product drop gets a post-mortem linked to its log rows. Each post-mortem includes the customer quotes the researcher heard, so the library holds the why behind each result, not only the numbers.

## Automate only the Instagram account, the payments and a custom link redirect

The free-data picture in October 2026 is lopsided. **Instagram is the one platform where emet can automate deep metrics today.** The Instagram API with Instagram Login works for Business or Creator accounts without a linked Facebook Page. **Standard Access for a business you own needs no App Review** ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/app-review)). Per-media metrics include views, reach, saves, shares, follows, profile visits, Reel average watch time and skip rate, and Story link clicks ([Meta Media Insights](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/reference/instagram-media/insights)). Four constraints shape the build. **`impressions` and `plays` were replaced by `views`** for all API versions from 21 April 2025 ([Meta v22 changelog](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/changelog/version22.0)). **Story insights disappear after 24 hours**, and data can lag up to 48 hours. The long-lived token lasts 60 days and must be refreshed before it expires ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/instagram-platform/reference/refresh_access_token.md)). Accounts under 100 followers get no follower demographics ([Meta Account Insights](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/api-reference/instagram-user/insights)). In practice, that means a daily Supabase cron that snapshots recent media into a history table (the API returns current values, not per-post time series), a Story capture inside 24 hours, and a token refresh every ~50 days. Whether Standard Access is limited to accounts held by people with an app role isn't stated in Meta's docs, so the team should check that in the App Dashboard before relying on it.

TikTok is the weak link. The **Display API returns only public counts** (views, likes, comments, shares) and needs Login Kit plus product approval, though a Sandbox allows testing on up to 10 accounts without review ([TikTok Display API](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/display-api-get-started); [TikTok Sandbox](https://developers.tiktok.com/blog/introducing-sandbox)). The **Research API is closed to commercial teams** ([TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/research-api)). It was unclear whether the organic Business API is free or self-serve for a small creator. Watch time, traffic source and followers gained per video should therefore come from a weekly manual entry from TikTok Studio. On sales, **Shopify is not €0** (Basic is €32/month after the trial) ([Shopify ES](https://www.shopify.com/es/precios)). **Stripe Payment Links charge only per transaction, 1.5% + €0.25 for standard EEA cards** ([Stripe](https://support.stripe.com/questions/pricing-updates-for-businesses-based-in-the-european-economic-area-(eea))). A webhook writing completed checkouts into Supabase, with promotion codes carrying channel attribution, is the cheapest automatic sales feed. Instagram native checkout isn't available in the EU, and TikTok Shop has run in Spain since December 2024 at a reported 9% commission ([TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktok-shop-expands-across-europe?lang=en-150)). For links, Bitly's free plan now has no click analytics and shows an interstitial ad ([qck.sh](https://qck.sh/blog/bitly-free-plan-limits)). Dub's free tier caps tracked events at 1,000/month with 30-day history ([Linkly](https://linklyhq.com/review/dub)). A **`/go/[slug]` route in Next.js that logs the click to Supabase and 302-redirects to a UTM-tagged URL** is free with no caps, and every stall QR code can point at it. **GA4's Data API allows 200,000 tokens per property per day**, far more than a daily pull needs ([Google](https://developers.google.com/analytics/devguides/reporting/data/v1/quotas)).

| Source | Route | Effort | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram own account | API now (Instagram Login, Standard Access), daily snapshot cron | Medium | High |
| Stripe Payment Links + promo codes | Webhook → Supabase | Low–medium | High |
| `/go/[slug]` redirect + QR codes | Build in-app | Low | High |
| Stall cash/Bizum sales | Mobile form, required HDYHAU and code | Low | High |
| GA4 | Data API once a site has traffic | Low–medium | High later |
| TikTok public counts | Display API (Sandbox first) or manual | Medium–high | Medium |
| TikTok watch time, sources, follows | Weekly manual entry | Low | Medium–high |
| Meta Ad Library, TikTok Creative Center | Manual swipe-file sessions | Low | Low–medium |
| Shopify, Fairing, Motion, Foreplay, Bitly/Dub paid | Skip (cost money or add nothing) | n/a | n/a |

## Keep decision numbers in 2D and use 3D only for overview

Perception research gives a clear ranking. Cleveland and McGill found people judge **position on a common scale most accurately, then length, then angle, area, and volume and colour last** ([Cornell notes](https://info3312.infosci.cornell.edu/ae/graphical-perception-A.html)). Adding perspective depth to bar charts lowers estimation accuracy ([Zacks et al.](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/reading-bar-graphs-effects-of-extraneous-depth-cues-and-graphical/)). One psychophysics study found people about **10% more accurate with 2D than 3D bars**, and needing larger differences before they noticed them in 3D ([University of Galway](https://research.universityofgalway.ie/en/publications/just-noticeable-differences-in-2d-and-3d-bar-charts-a-psychophysi-3/)). The penalty isn't universal, since some studies find no significant difference ([MDPI 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-6470/4/4/46)). Munzner's rule, "no unjustified 3D", reserves 3D for truly spatial data or shape perception with interactive navigation, and warns that perspective "interferes with all size channel encodings" ([Munzner](https://cs.ubc.ca/~tmm/talks/vad/VAD-rules.pdf)). The case *for* a distinctive visual rests on memorability, not accuracy. Borkin and colleagues found colour, recognisable objects and unusual forms make charts more memorable ([Harvard VCG](https://vcg.seas.harvard.edu/publications/20130101-what-makes-a-visualization-memorable)). Their follow-up found that **good titles and annotations drive accurate recall** more than form does ([World Bank summary](https://blogs.worldbank.org/opendata/what-makes-data-visualization-memorable)).

That evidence supports a split design. emet's **3D revenue mountain is justified as identity and overview**, and so is an optional **content landscape** where each post is a peak (height = reach, colour lightness = share-plus-save rate on a Viridis-style colour-blind-safe scale). Neither should be where anyone reads or compares an exact number. The good 3D precedents work the same way. GitHub's globe puts exact pull-request details in hover cards, falls back to lower quality tiers when frame rate drops below 55.5 FPS, and shows an SVG placeholder until the canvas is ready ([GitHub](https://github.blog/engineering/engineering-principles/how-we-built-the-github-globe/)). The NYT's 3D yield curve used computed camera presets and became a stepper of static views on mobile ([Data Stories](https://datastori.es/data-stories-tv-00-the-nyt-3d-yeld-curve-chart-w-gregor-aisch/)). So the rules are: tapping a peak opens a 2D card with the thumbnail and exact numbers; phones get camera presets, not free orbit; reduced-motion users get a static fallback; and following Esri's "one map per phone view" guidance, **no more than one 3D scene per screen** ([Esri](https://enterprise.arcgis.com/en/dashboards/latest/reference/dashboards-on-your-smartphone.htm)). No controlled study has tested terrain metaphors for business metrics, so the landscape is a reasonable bet, not a proven technique.

Everything the team acts on stays 2D, following patterns mature tools already use. **Goal pacing uses Stephen Few's bullet graph**: actual against target and expected-to-date over muted bands, with a reversed scale for "not to exceed" metrics like CAC ([BetterEvaluation](https://www.betterevaluation.org/methods-approaches/methods/bullet-graph); [Perceptual Edge](https://www.perceptualedge.com/blog/?p=217)). Bullet graphs fit profit, revenue and posts shipped, not follower counts. The **funnel is horizontal stepped bars** (views → profile visits/link taps → DMs → orders) with step conversion rates and the biggest drop highlighted, the standard funnel form in tools like Mixpanel ([Mixpanel](https://mintlify.com/mixpanel/docs/reports/overview)). Channel mix is sorted horizontal bars. The **creative leaderboard is Motion's thumbnail tile grid**, each tile with a few metrics, a sparkline and a winner badge, because marketers recognise a post by its image ([Motion](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/7090459-how-to-build-a-report)). KPI cards combine a big number, change versus last period, a Tufte sparkline ("word-sized graphics") and a one-line annotation, above a timeline with launches and stall days marked as events ([Tufte](https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/)). Repeat purchases suit a cohort heatmap once there are a few months of data. Hook tests suit an interval-against-zero card with Amplitude's three plain verdicts ("not enough evidence yet", "likely better", "likely worse") ([Amplitude](https://amplitude.com/docs/feature-experiment/analysis-view)). Optionally, hypothetical outcome plots, which animate possible outcomes and help non-experts grasp uncertainty better than error bars, can be added ([MU Collective](https://mucollective.northwestern.edu/project/hops)). Status uses arrows plus blue/orange at different lightness rather than red/green alone, since red-green deficiency affects about **1 in 12 men** ([Datawrapper](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/colorblindness-part1)). On phones, following NN/g's definition of dashboards as at-a-glance displays and the vendor mobile guidance, KPIs stack first, one chart per card, with at most three filters ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dashboards-preattentive/); [Tableau](https://www.tableau.com/blog/mobile-dashboard-design-less-more-small-screen-47854)).

## Conclusion

The research says less about tools than about **logging discipline**. Every recommended metric is a simple sum over three tables emet controls: orders (with a required channel answer, code and customer ID), content (with tags and a decision), and stall sessions. The real risk is not missing data sources but inconsistent entry, so the highest-leverage dashboard features are mundane: required dropdowns, a 24-hour sales-logging rule, metrics locked when the week closes, and a visible disagreement rate between attribution signals. The 50% margin implied by the goals also sets a hard rule in advance. Paid promotion is only worth it at a MER above 2.0, and the dashboard can compute that from the first boosted post.

Two honest limits shape what comes next. Published benchmarks barely fit a brand this small: there are no sourced norms for DM-to-order rates, stall revenue per hour or content hit rates, and engagement benchmarks span more than tenfold. emet's first 4–6 weeks are for building its own baselines, not for hitting borrowed targets. And the evidence on how famous brands run marketing internally is thinner than its reputation. What can be verified comes from templates and tool vendors, not from Nike or Glossier. The advantage for a student team is that the parts that matter (one approver, one log with decisions, one fixed weekly scorecard) cost nothing and can start this Monday.
