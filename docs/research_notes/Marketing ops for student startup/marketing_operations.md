# Marketing operations: briefs, rituals, experiments, learnings libraries, per-role metrics (and fit for emet)

Evidence note: most sources I could find are templates or vendor material (HubSpot via Atlassian, Asana, Motion, Foreplay, Dropbox, Atlassian), not peer-reviewed research. Vendor-reported numbers are labelled as such. I found no primary 2022–2026 documentation of the internal brief templates or rituals of Nike, P&G, Red Bull, Airbnb, Glossier, Notion or Linear, so anything about those brands' internal processes is a gap, not a finding.

## 1. Campaign / creative brief templates: fields, handoff, turnaround

### Takeaway
Published brief templates agree on a core: named roles (driver/approver), goal, audience, single key message plus a reason to believe, tone, a deliverables table (deliverable, channel, specs, owner), and dates/approval steps. Handoff in mature teams goes through one standard intake form into one queue that a single person triages. I found no published turnaround SLAs from the named brands; the only figure is vendor-reported.

### Cited Findings
- HubSpot's creative brief template (mirrored by Atlassian Confluence) has these sections. **Header:** roles (driver, approver, contributors, informed), campaign name, timeline, creative request due date, status. **Project overview:** goal, opportunities, challenges, competitive environment, selling points, value propositions. **Objectives and target audience**, each with a High/Medium/Low priority. **Creative direction:** brand strategy, creative requirements, concept statement, pre- and post-creative behaviour (what the audience thinks or does before and after seeing the work). **Messaging and tone:** key message, reason to believe, tone of voice. **Execution:** a deliverables table (deliverable, channel, specifications, owner), budget, timeline, review and approval process. — [Atlassian: Creative brief template](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/creative-brief)
- HubSpot offers separate brief templates for simple campaigns and for video projects (the video fields were not visible in my sources). — [Small Business Trends](https://smallbiztrends.com/creative-brief-examples/)
- Asana's own brand creative team collects requests through one custom form with these fields: type of creative support, due date, target audience, project scope, stakeholders and their responsibilities (as a RACI), and design specs. Requests go into a single project where a Head of Creative Operations reviews, prioritises and assigns them using workload dashboards. Automations set due dates, assign review tasks and route approvals. Feedback is left on the asset attached to the task. **No turnaround SLA is stated.** — [Asana on Asana: creative production](https://asana.com/resources/asana-on-asana-creative-production.md)
- Asana's template guidance (opinion/vendor) says a standard request form "provides the necessary information to scope the work, set expectations, and complete the project without miscommunication", and that missing details delay work. — [Asana creative requests template](https://asana.com/templates/creative-requests)
- Vendor-reported case study: Stance's creative team cut production time by 50% after moving to a standard intake form plus mapped approvals. The study does not separate how much came from the form. ClassPass reports launching campaigns 30–40% faster, also vendor-reported. — [Asana: Stance](https://asana.com/case-study/stance-creative-production); [Asana: ClassPass](https://asana.com/case-study/classpass.md)
- Gymshark: the in-house creative studio grew from 8 creatives to 70+ people in 11 teams (creative, copy, design, video, production and more) under a Head of Creative Studio who joined in 2018. A Senior Social Content Executive job listing asks for "experience writing briefs for content creators", so briefing creators is a named, owned skill. — [D&AD profile: Ashley Wilson](https://www.dandad.org/profiles/person/606123/ashley-wilson/); [Gymshark Greenhouse listing](https://job-boards.eu.greenhouse.io/gymshark/jobs/4644967101)
- Creative-strategy tooling: Foreplay briefs have modular fields (format, aspect ratios, platforms, assets, music) and can embed saved reference ads ("swipe file" items) directly in the brief. This is vendor-described; an independent review says the tool researches, organises and briefs but does not produce creative. — [Foreplay](https://www.foreplay.co); [Superscale review](https://superscale.ai/alternatives/foreplay/review)

### Inferences
- For short-form social, the useful brief adds two things to the classic HubSpot fields: reference examples embedded in the brief (the Foreplay pattern) and the hypothesis or hook being tested. Without these, a brief can't feed an experiment log.
- The common pattern ("one form, one queue, one triager") matters more than any specific tool. The time savings come from requests arriving complete.

### Gaps
- I found no primary, dated (2022–2026) internal brief templates or SLAs from Nike, P&G, Red Bull, Airbnb, Glossier, Notion or Linear. Search returned only aggregators. Any "Nike brief" fields circulating online should be treated as unverified.
- I found no published benchmark for creative turnaround times in small teams.

## 2. Planning rituals: standups, reviews, retros, OKRs, calendars, approvals

### Takeaway
The best-documented approval tool is DACI: one Driver, exactly one Approver, Contributors with a voice but no vote, and Informed. Atlassian sizes it at 3–6 people and about an hour. Evidence for specific weekly or monthly marketing rituals at named companies was thin.

### Cited Findings
- DACI roles. **Driver** coordinates and makes sure a decision happens by the agreed date. **Approver** makes the final call, and "there must be only one". **Contributors** have "a voice but no vote". **Informed** are told the outcome afterwards. Template fields: status, impact, people involved, due date, outcome, background (1–2 sentences), relevant data, decision factors, options considered (pros, cons, cost), action items. Recommended for 3–6 participants, 15 minutes of prep and about 60 minutes of run time. Atlassian advises recording the final decision so later teams know how and why it was made. — [Atlassian Team Playbook: DACI](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook/plays/daci)
- HubSpot's brief puts DACI-style roles (driver, approver, contributors, informed) in the brief header, so approvals are decided per campaign before work starts. — [Atlassian/HubSpot brief](https://www.atlassian.com/software/confluence/templates/creative-brief)
- Asana's creative team records stakeholder responsibilities as a RACI on each intake request. — [Asana on Asana](https://asana.com/resources/asana-on-asana-creative-production.md)
- Glossier gathered customer input continuously. It reportedly ran a Slack channel for about 100 top customers to give feedback on releases (one secondary source says it is now defunct). The Milky Jelly Cleanser was shaped by more than 400 Into The Gloss comments, with the chemist tracking repeated words ("moist", "glowy", "mild"). A former head of customer experience noted the trade-off: community co-creation makes 3–5 year planning harder. All of these are secondary sources. — [MBS Group](https://www.thembsgroup.co.uk/internal/glossier-beautiful-on-the-inside-too/); [Harvard D3 student case](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/?p=7413)

### Inferences
- For a 5-person team, DACI's "one Approver" rule is the most useful part. It stops a 15-person startup from turning every post into a group vote.
- The Glossier evidence suggests the market researcher's main input should be a regular, structured flow of customer language (comments, stall conversations) into briefs. This should be continuous, not a one-off survey.

### Gaps
- I found no primary sources (2022–2026) describing weekly marketing standups, monthly retros or marketing OKRs at Nike, P&G, Red Bull, Airbnb, Notion or Linear. A Lenny's Newsletter search returned nothing on team rituals.
- I found no evidence comparing OKRs with simpler goal-setting for very small marketing teams.

## 3. Creative testing and experimentation

### Takeaway
Practitioners test one variable at a time (usually the hook), tag every asset with a fixed taxonomy so results can be grouped, and roll performance up to the concept rather than to each variation. Hook-rate benchmarks come from vendors and conflict with each other. True A/B significance isn't achievable in organic social.

### Cited Findings
- Motion (creative analytics) naming-convention fields, each with a prefix:
  - `s:` source
  - `as:` format/concept (UGC mashup, unboxing, street interview)
  - `m:` messaging (FOMO, founder story)
  - `h:` hook (e.g. "3 reasons why", shocking)
  - `p:` product (with a "multi" value)
  - `i:` talent
  - `gen:` and `age:` talent gender and age
  - `len:` length
  - `o:` offer
  - `lp:` landing page
  - `a:` audio (music or voiceover)

  Motion also advises splitting the hook into **hook visual** and **hook messaging**. Its stated principles: include only actionable fields ("the most important" principle); use neither too few nor too many fields; build names from dropdowns tied to a shared glossary; brainstorm the fields with the creative team first. — [Motion: Build a naming convention for success (Dec 2024)](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/build-a-naming-convention-for-success/)
- Taxonomy advice (vendor/opinion): keep the required tag set to 5–6 fields. Hook type is usually the strongest performance signal. Track the concept separately from its variations so performance rolls up to the concept. — [Linkrunner: creative tagging taxonomy](https://deeplinks.linkrunner.io/blog/creative-tagging-taxonomy-that-scales)
- Caveat: names only capture what someone typed at launch, and people tend to fill in only the easy fields. — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/how-to-tag-ad-creatives-automatically)
- Hook metrics are defined differently by platform. TikTok uses roughly 2-second views divided by impressions. Meta uses a 3-second threshold. Some definitions divide by plays instead of impressions. Benchmarks conflict: average hook rate of about 25–30% (top 40–50%) in one vendor source, 20–25% in another. Neither is a controlled study. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate); [Segwise glossary](https://segwise.ai/glossary/hook-rate); [Motion metrics cheat sheet](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/7730931-metrics-cheat-sheet)
- The claim that "63% of successful TikTok ads convey the core message in the first 3 seconds" and the "60% higher retention" claim are widely repeated, but I could not trace either to a primary study. Treat both as unverified. — [Segwise frame-by-frame](https://segwise.ai/blog/anatomy-top-performing-video-ad-frame-by-frame); [Virlo](https://virlo.ai/blog/why-the-first-few-seconds-matter)
- Statistical significance is out of reach in organic social because there is no stable traffic split. Practitioners advise varying only the opening and holding the rest of the creative constant. — [blckalpaca knowledge base](https://blckalpaca.at/en/knowledge-base/social-media/social-media-content-creation-formats/hook-rate-retention-watch-time)
- Google's ABCD framework (Attention, Branding, Connection, Direction) was validated by Google and Kantar over about 11,000 ads and 180 creative features. Ads following the ABCDs were linked to a 30% higher likelihood of short-term sales lift and 17% greater long-term brand contribution. These figures are self-reported by the framework's owners, from an April 2021 study, which is older than the requested window. — [Kantar case study](https://www.kantar.com/industries/technology-and-telecoms/validating-googles-abcd-framework-with-the-power-of-artificial-intelligence); [Think with Google](https://thinkwithgoogle.com/consumer-insights/consumer-trends/trending-visual-stories/story/storytelling-techniques-on-youtube)
- Experiment-log templates (all generic or vendor) share these fields: a stable ID, hypothesis in if-then form, success metric and criteria, duration, method, results, learnings and next test, with status and decision chosen from fixed lists. — [Appcues growth experiment template](https://appcues.com/templates/growth-experiment-template); [Obvious: growth hypothesis framework](https://obvious.ai/templates/growth-hypothesis-testing-framework); [Makeform A/B test log](https://www.makeform.ai/tools/ai-a-b-test-log-form-generator)

### Inferences
- With €0 budget and organic-only distribution, emet can't run clean A/B tests. The practical alternative is to post several hook variants of the same body over a week and compare the platform's 2–3 second retention and saves, accepting that the result is directional.
- An explicit **decision** field (scale / iterate / kill) is often missing from generic templates. Adding it is what turns a log into a playbook.

### Gaps
- No Reforge-specific experiment template was retrievable.
- I found no peer-reviewed evidence on hook testing in organic (unpaid) short-form video.

## 4. Knowledge bases of learnings: post-mortems, swipe files, playbooks, tagging

### Takeaway
A reusable "what works" base has three parts: (a) a post-mortem stored with each campaign record, with a summary at the top; (b) a swipe file of saved reference creatives, tagged; (c) a consistent tag taxonomy shared by your own posts and the swipe file, so you can filter by format, hook, product, audience or channel.

### Cited Findings
- Dropbox's campaign post-mortem template covers: name, dates, owner, goal, audience, channels, results, what worked, what didn't, root causes, decisions, action items. It puts a short summary at the top (goal, outcome, key learnings, next steps) and says the document should "live with the campaign record, not in a one-off deck that disappears after the meeting." Its stated purpose is to tell a future team what to repeat, avoid or test. — [Dropbox: campaign post-mortem](https://www.dropbox.com/resources/campaign-post-mortem)
- Post-mortems should compare results with the targets set before launch and look for root causes. — [Product Marketing Alliance launch post-mortem template](https://productmarketingalliance.com/content/files/2025/01/Product-launch-post-mortem-template.pdf); [The Cirqle](https://thecirqle.com/blog-post/campaign-post-mortems-the-step-most-brands-skip-and-why-it-costs-them)
- Teams often keep learnings to themselves; Zappi advises sharing them even when a campaign underperformed. — [Zappi](https://www.zappi.io/web/blog/advertising-postmortems-make-next-campaign-better)
- Foreplay's swipe file saves ads from the Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center with tags and share links, and its Lens analytics feature is meant to show which patterns work so they can become new briefs (vendor-described). Third-party pricing of $59–459/month is unconfirmed. — [Foreplay](https://www.foreplay.co); [GTM Directory](https://thegtmdirectory.com/tools/foreplay/md)
- Motion's glossary-plus-dropdown approach keeps tag values consistent across collaborators, and property-based grouping picks up new tag values automatically. — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/build-a-naming-convention-for-success/)

### Inferences
- The free equivalent of Motion and Foreplay is a single Notion or Airtable database (or a Google Sheet) with dropdown tag fields. A "Library" view filtered to Decision = Scale becomes the playbook. Free tools such as the Meta Ad Library and TikTok Creative Center can stock the swipe file.

### Gaps
- I found no first-party write-ups (2022–2026) of how Gymshark, Glossier, Red Bull or Nike structure internal creative libraries.

## 5. Handing metrics to each role: scorecards and avoiding vanity work

### Takeaway
I found little direct evidence. The usable principles from the sources: each metric needs one owner, metrics must lead to an action (Motion's "actionable" rule), and results should be compared with targets set before launch.

### Cited Findings
- Only track elements that are actionable. — [Motion talk](https://motionapp.com/library/talk/build-a-naming-convention-for-success/)
- Compare actual results with pre-launch targets. — [Product Marketing Alliance template](https://productmarketingalliance.com/content/files/2025/01/Product-launch-post-mortem-template.pdf); [The Cirqle](https://thecirqle.com/blog-post/campaign-post-mortems-the-step-most-brands-skip-and-why-it-costs-them)
- Asana's creative team uses workload dashboards to see who has capacity. — [Asana on Asana](https://asana.com/resources/asana-on-asana-creative-production.md)
- Hook rate (2–3 second views divided by impressions) and hold rate (Meta's 15s/3s retention; TikTok's 6-second hold) are the standard creative-level metrics. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/hook-rate-to-hold-rate); [Motion metrics cheat sheet](https://help.motionapp.com/en/articles/7730931-metrics-cheat-sheet)

### Inferences
- A vanity metric is one that no role can act on. Follower count alone fits that description; hook rate (for the creator), saves and shares (for social) and units sold per stall-hour (for the leader) each point to a specific action.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced examples of per-role marketing scorecards from the named companies.

## Fit for emet

### Takeaway
Adopt one brief, one combined content-and-experiment log that doubles as the tagged library, one post-mortem per campaign or stall day, one shared tag glossary, and three rituals (weekly 30-minute review, monthly 60-minute retro, DACI-style single approver). Everything should run in a free Notion or Google Sheet workspace. Everything below is my recommendation, built on the sourced patterns above; none of it is a cited finding.

### Cited Findings (basis)
- Brief fields: HubSpot/Atlassian template. Intake form plus one triager: Asana. Single approver: Atlassian DACI. Prefixed tags, split hook, glossary, 5–6 required tags, concept above variation: Motion and Linkrunner. Post-mortem stored with the record and summary on top: Dropbox. Organic tests are directional only: blckalpaca. (All links above.)

### Inferences (recommended adaptation for a 5-person team, €0 budget)

**Roles and ownership. The marketing leader is the single Approver for everything.**

| Role | Owns | Weekly scorecard (one owner per number) |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing leader | Goals, approves briefs and posts (one approver), runs rituals, writes post-mortems | Units sold (online plus stall), sales per stall-hour, number of experiments with a recorded decision |
| Social media manager | Calendar, posting, community replies, experiment log entries, swipe-file curation | Saves plus shares per post, profile-visit-to-DM/order count, posts shipped vs planned |
| Content creator | Concepts, hooks, scripts, on-camera, stall content | Hook rate (2–3 s views ÷ views) per hook variant, number of hook variants tested |
| Editor & designer | Editing, thumbnails, covers, stall signage, brand consistency; keeps the tag glossary | Turnaround vs agreed date, hold rate per edit style |
| Market researcher | Customer-language bank (comments, DMs, stall conversations), competitor swipe file, reads results | Insights logged per week, share of briefs that cite an insight |

**Artefact 1: Brief (one page; the requester fills it in, the leader approves; written at least 3 days before the post date).** Fields:
1. ID and campaign name
2. Driver / Approver (always the leader) / Contributors
3. Goal (one measurable target, e.g. "sell 20 units of X")
4. Audience (one sentence)
5. Customer insight or quote (from the researcher)
6. Single key message
7. Reason to believe
8. Hypothesis ("If we open with <hook>, then <metric> beats <baseline>")
9. Hooks to test (2–3 variants of the same body)
10. Deliverables table: asset, channel (IG / TikTok / stall), format, aspect ratio, length, owner, due date
11. Reference examples (links from the swipe file)
12. Tags pre-filled from the glossary
13. Tone
14. Mandatories (price, handle, stall time and place)
15. Status

Suggested internal turnaround (my recommendation; no benchmark exists): editor gets the raw footage at least 48 hours before posting, with one feedback round plus one final check.

**Artefact 2: Content and experiment log, which is also the library (one row per published asset; the social media manager adds the row, the leader enters the decision).** Fields:
- ID
- Concept ID (groups variants of the same concept)
- Brief ID
- Post date
- Channel
- Link
- Tags (see artefact 4)
- Hypothesis
- Variable changed (only one)
- Metrics at 48 hours and 7 days: views, hook rate, average watch time or hold, saves, shares, comments, DMs or orders attributed, stall sales if relevant
- Baseline (median of the last 10 posts)
- Result (win / flat / loss)
- Learning (one sentence)
- Decision (scale = make more like this; iterate = change one thing; kill)
- Next action

The "Library" view filters to Decision = Scale and can be grouped by hook type, format or product.

**Artefact 3: Post-mortem (per campaign, product drop or stall day; the leader writes it within 7 days and stores it with the campaign record).** Fields:
- Summary on top: goal, outcome, top 3 learnings, next steps
- Name, dates, owner
- Goal vs actual
- Channels and assets (linked log rows)
- What worked
- What didn't
- Root cause
- Decisions (repeat / avoid / test)
- Action items with owners
- Customer quotes heard (from the researcher)

**Artefact 4: Tag glossary (owned by the editor & designer; dropdown values only).** Six required tags:
- `p:` product
- `f:` format (talking-head, UGC demo, unboxing, before/after, street or stall interview, carousel, static)
- `hv:` hook visual (product close-up, face-to-camera, text-on-screen, action-in-motion)
- `hm:` hook message (problem, question, price/offer, social proof, founder/student story, "3 reasons")
- `aud:` audience (e.g. students, gift buyers)
- `ch:` channel (IG Reel, IG Story, TikTok, stall)

Optional tags: `o:` offer, `a:` audio (trend sound, voiceover, music), `t:` talent.

**Rituals:**
- **Weekly review (30 min, Monday, all 5; the leader chairs):**
  - 10 min: last week's log rows, deciding scale / iterate / kill
  - 10 min: approve this week's briefs and calendar
  - 5 min: researcher's insights
  - 5 min: blockers
- **Monthly retro (60 min):**
  - Post-mortems for that month's campaigns and stalls
  - Update the Library view and the tag glossary
  - Set next month's 1–2 marketing goals (plain targets rather than full OKRs)
- **Async daily check-in (optional):** one line each in the group chat.
- **Approvals:** DACI with the leader as the only Approver. Anyone else on the 15-person team is Informed, or a Contributor if asked. Every brief carries its own DACI line.

### Gaps
- None of these field lists or durations have been validated on student teams. Review them after the first month.
